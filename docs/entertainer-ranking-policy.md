# Entertainer Ranking Policy

## Status

**Selected client direction with unresolved configuration — formal policy values require CEO or General Manager approval before activation.**

## Purpose

Use a fair, explainable four-rank system that combines verified evidence with authorized human judgment. The system calculates evidence and promotion readiness; it does not make the final rank decision automatically.

## Four ranks

1. Bronze
2. Silver
3. Gold
4. Diamond

The current public rank may be shown on the approved customer-facing entertainer profile. Confidential scores, incidents, financial information, and decision evidence remain internal.

## Starting rank

New entertainers begin at **Gold** under the latest client direction.

This replaces the earlier onboarding/provisional starting-rank assumption. A later rank increase or decrease follows the approved evaluation, grace, evidence, and human-decision process.

## Evidence dimensions

### 1. Sales and performance

Verified eligible sales/performance is a major measurable component. Exact eligible sources, rank thresholds, and weights are **TBD — Business configuration required**.

### 2. Attendance and reliability

Evidence may include attendance, late arrivals, no-shows, and nights worked per week. Approximately four or more nights per week is an interview input, not an approved constant.

### 3. Customer loyalty

Use traceable repeat-customer and repeat-reservation behavior. A customer repeatedly requesting or visiting with the same entertainer is a loyalty indicator. The qualifying interaction count and evaluation window are **TBD — Business configuration required**.

### 4. Verified behavioral record

Managers may record structured operational or misconduct incidents. An incident includes category, description, date/time, branch, reporting manager, severity if used, supporting evidence/reference, resolution, and status.

A submitted complaint is not automatically a verified incident. Management review must determine whether it becomes ranking evidence.

## Evaluation and decision lifecycle

1. Collect authorized sales, attendance, reliability, loyalty, feedback, incident, training, and compliance evidence.
2. At the configured cadence, calculate the approved metrics using the effective policy version.
3. Produce an explainable recommendation such as Promotion ready or Not yet promotion ready.
4. Show which criteria are satisfied, unmet, excluded, or affected by missing data.
5. Route the recommendation and evidence to the authorized decision maker.
6. The human approver accepts, rejects, or overrides the recommendation with a reason.
7. Apply the approved rank on its effective date.
8. Preserve recommendation, source evidence, policy version, approver, final decision, override reason, previous/new rank, and effective date.
9. Provide a review, appeal, adjustment, and audit path.

## Promotion and demotion controls

- A rank change is never performed solely by AI.
- Missing data must be disclosed rather than treated silently as positive performance.
- Promotion requires the configured minimum evidence and approved gates.
- Demotion must not result from one weak period alone unless an approved hard gate applies.
- Repeated unresolved no-shows may trigger an approved hard gate or benefit suspension.
- Sustained sales decline may support demotion only under an approved evaluation and grace rule.
- Manual overrides are restricted and audited.

## Diamond interview inputs

Current interview inputs mention approximately three months of qualifying performance and recruiting or bringing approximately nine new entertainers. These are not approved constants.

## Financial relationship

The effective approved rank may select the applicable customer-time/hourly revenue-share policy. Proposed rank shares are documented in [Loans and Settlement Requirements](loans-and-settlement-requirements.md). A rank change must not rewrite already finalized settlements.

## Entertainer explanation and coaching

The Entertainer PWA and personal assistant may explain:

- current rank and effective date;
- objective evidence used;
- satisfied and unmet criteria;
- late/no-show counts and attendance trends;
- progress toward promotion;
- approved coaching focus;
- next review date.

The assistant must distinguish objective system evidence from management judgment and must not expose another entertainer's data.

## Approval required

CEO or General Manager approval is required for:

- metric definitions, weights, thresholds, and data-quality rules;
- work-night and loyalty qualification;
- evaluation window and cadence;
- Diamond conditions;
- hard gates, grace, promotion, and demotion;
- rank decision authority and any required CEO approval;
- benefits and financial effects;
- exception, appeal, and override rules.
